## Status-and-scope

This is Patchwork’s **methodological alignment and compatibility crosswalk**, not an ENISA publication, certification, endorsement, regulatory compliance assessment or claim of formal conformance. ENISA’s *Cybersecurity Threat Landscape Methodology* is a production methodology; its annual threat landscape is an example of a finished product, not a mandatory universal report schema. The methodology itself describes a high-level, evolving approach intended to help other entities produce their own reports ([methodology §3](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#3-future-work>)); its [legal notice](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#legal-notice>) does not establish a regulatory obligation.

ENISA is a **complementary production-method layer**. It does not retroactively become one of the five foundational sources composed in [framework §2](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#2-source-standards-and-how-they-compose>): CERT-EU, Mandiant / Google Threat Intelligence, Palo Alto Unit 42, CISA Automated Indicator Sharing and OASIS STIX 2.1. Those sources retain their existing roles, history and precedence. MITRE ATT&CK is used for techniques; that does not replace a member of this historical five-source list.

The adoption boundary is:

- **Core compatibility constraints remain binding:** existing attribution, per-observation grading, taxonomy, handling, identity and serialisation rules are not replaced by the ENISA method. See [framework §§4–9](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#4-adversary-model-and-the-attribution-ladder>).
- **Adopted production practices:** audience-led requirements, documented collection and curation, explicit limitations, human analytical accountability, review, dissemination and an actionable feedback loop. Their application is recorded in the report, not inferred from using a heading.
- **Optional annual profile:** [annual-threat-landscape](<Report-Templates/annual-threat-landscape.md>) is a more detailed Markdown profile of the existing [net-assessment](<Report-Templates/net-assessment.md>) type. A short retrospective can still use the base template. Selecting the profile does not change the report type or make every Patchwork report an annual landscape.
- **No data migration or wire-format change:** no new `report_types` value, enum, STIX object type, STIX property or Patchwork extension property is introduced. The profile uses `report_types: ["net-assessment"]`. Methodology, denominators, review records, release dates and feedback records are report prose/tables or existing references, not new structured fields. Existing extension version and identity keys remain unchanged ([framework §9.3](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#93-classification-properties>)).

## Analytical-constraints

### Relevance-urgency-confidence-and-probability

Keep four questions separate:

1. **Relevance:** Does the evidence answer the declared intelligence requirement for this audience? ENISA triages by source confidence and EU relevance ([methodology §2.3.3](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#233-input-data-collection>)). Patchwork localises relevance to the member’s declared constituency and [relational threat-domain ladder](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#33-threat-domains>). No ENISA relevance score or numeric conversion is adopted.
2. **Urgency/proximity:** How close and urgent is the activity? Existing `High` / `Medium` / `Low` [threat levels](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#34-threat-levels>) remain separate from sourcing. EU relevance or high observed volume does not automatically imply `High`.
3. **Evidence quality / confidence:** How reliable is this source and how credible is this observation? ENISA’s [Admiralty table](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#source-confidence-table>) is compatible with Patchwork’s existing A–F × 1–6 approach. Grade each observation, preserve conflicting or unjudgeable evidence and explain assessment confidence separately. A source list grade or report-level summary cannot replace event-level grading. `F` and `6` mean cannot be judged, not proven false. Use only the existing [serialisation lookup and object-dependent meaning of confidence](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#71-admiralty-grading-attaches-to-the-observation>); do not average a dataset into an adversary reliability grade.
4. **Estimative probability:** How likely is the proposition or future outcome? Annual p.10 uses words of estimative probability to distinguish external reporting from ENISA assessments. Appendix B (printed/PDF p.100) labels its matrix “CONFIDENCE LEVEL” and describes HIGHLY LIKELY >90%, LIKELY 60–90%, POSSIBLE 40–60%, PLAUSIBLE 10–40%, UNLIKELY <10%. Preserve that terminology when describing the source, but **do not equate those likelihood bands with Admiralty grades, assessment confidence or STIX `confidence`**. No automatic mapping or new local scale is adopted, and no endpoint rule is invented where the printed percentage bands meet. Any report-specific likelihood wording must define its use in prose and remain separate from evidence confidence. [Framework §7.2](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#72-comparison-with-the-other-standards-confidence-scales>) still records FIRST LCA / WEP as not adopted.

### Taxonomy-counting-and-unknowns

ENISA calls for consistent taxonomies and suitable analytical frameworks ([§2.4.3](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#243-cyber-threat-taxonomy>), [§2.4.4](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#244-cti-frameworks>)); that does not mandate replacing Patchwork’s [sector hierarchy](<Sectors.md>), [activity categories](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#52-activity-categories>) or [ATT&CK representation](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#62-techniques>).

- Preserve source labels, explain mapping into local sectors/categories and identify unmapped or ambiguous cases. Annual source chapter titles such as cybercrime, State-nexus, hacktivism and FIMI are editorial groupings, not new controlled values or a complete one-to-one mapping. Do not force FIMI into an unsupported `intent` value ([known gap in framework §2.5](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#25-not-adopted>)).
- Define the unit of analysis: incident, organisation, claim, campaign, vulnerability, technique observation or report. Deduplicate underlying events rather than counting repeated vendor reporting as independent corroboration. For multi-sector victims, state whether the chart uses one primary sector, multiple assignments or a documented unknown bucket; explain totals that are not additive. A presentation bucket labelled “unknown” is not a newly minted sector identity or vocabulary value.
- Every percentage, rank and comparison needs its population, numerator, denominator, period, geographic scope, source corpus, exclusions and missingness where applicable. Identify overlapping categories and changing coverage; distinguish absolute counts from shares. State what “top” means and how ties are handled. Do not interpret visibility as prevalence, claim volume as verified impact, or missing telemetry as zero incidents.
- Annual printed/PDF pp.9–10 explicitly discuss incomplete OSINT and voluntary reporting, vague geography/sector labels, multi-sector curation, overlapping cybercrime activity categories and delayed espionage reporting. They warn that increased reporting need not mean increased activity. Preserve these as constraints on inference, not merely boilerplate.
- Do not mix independent corpora under one denominator: the annual source distinguishes its main incident dataset (p.9), NIS2 reporting with an as-of date of 14 July 2026 (p.19), and the EEAS-derived FIMI material (pp.9, 74). Reusable reports must identify their own populations rather than inherit those source facts. The annual p.9 calls the EEAS input “FIMI Threat Landscape 2025,” while p.74 describes the fourth EEAS report published in March 2026 and covering 2025. Preserve these differing source locators and verify the edition before reuse rather than silently harmonising them; see [edition and period cautions](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-threat-landscape-2026-schema.md#edition-and-period-cautions>).

### Attribution-and-human-analysis

Correlation and enrichment may add context ([methodology §2.4.6](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#246-correlate-and-enrich-processed-content>)); they do not supply attribution evidence by themselves. Preserve the distinction between a source’s claim, an observed fact and the member’s assessment. State-nexus language, geopolitical drivers or an actor’s public claim do not establish sponsorship, control, identity or promotion. Patchwork retains [technical attribution doctrine](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#45-attribution-doctrine>), [separate linked tiers](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#41-three-tiers-of-object-linked>), [persona separation](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#44-personas-are-objects-not-aliases>) and [multiple-claimant safeguards](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#48-when-several-actors-claim-one-activity>).

The adopted ENISA-method boundary follows [§2.4.7](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#247-use-of-ai-supported-tools>): AI-supported monitoring/collection and limited editorial assistance may be used with human source validation, handling controls and quality review. **AI must not identify trends, draw analytical conclusions or formulate the landscape’s assessments.** Human analysts select and validate evidence, choose the analytical approach, interpret results and approve final analytical content. General discussion of automated or hybrid methods in §2.5 does not override this explicit AI restriction. Automation of counting or format conversion is not delegated analytical judgment; humans validate the inputs, rules and interpretation.

Record actual tool use, boundaries and human checks; do not copy ENISA’s declaration as if it described the member’s process. The annual PDF’s p.4 disclaimer says AI supported limited collection/processing, experts reviewed outputs and no AI-generated content was used in that report. That source-specific statement is not permission for AI-authored assessments or evidence that Patchwork reports used no AI. Consider alternatives and inconsistent evidence as required by [methodology §2.5.2](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#252-performing-analysis>).

### Chronology-and-stable-report-identity

Keep the edition label, observation/incident date, recorded-date selection rule, reporting window, collection cut-off, source publication date, actual release date and revision date distinguishable. A named annual edition does not imply the same calendar year for the incident dataset.

The supplied annual source is titled **2026** and dated **September 2026**. Its methodology (printed/PDF p.9) explicitly selects incidents **recorded from 1 January to 31 December 2025**, with a six-month overlap with the previous ETL after a move to calendar-year coverage. This is an explained edition/window distinction, not a reason to silently relabel the source “2025” or claim an unexplained contradictory date. Later contextual references and a separately dated NIS2 corpus must not silently enlarge that main dataset’s window. The methodology conversion also preserves older “2025” wording in its September 2026 source; see its [conversion notes](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-ctl-methodology-2026.md#conversion-notes>).

For a **new Patchwork recurring net assessment**, use the first day of the declared period as the stable `Published` / STIX `published` anchor, as the [base template already requires](<Report-Templates/net-assessment.md#period-covered>). With a 2025 calendar-year window the anchor is 2025-01-01, even if the edition or release is in 2026. Record the actual release date separately in report prose. Keep the namespaced title and anchor unchanged across revisions; existing [name + published identity](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#92-identity-and-merge-rules>) and STIX versioning still apply. This is a local recurring-report convention, not a claim that ENISA assigned that STIX timestamp to its publication. Do not retroactively re-key previously ingested source reports or migrate old records under this guidance.

State whether inclusion uses recorded, observed, incident or disclosure dates, including start/end boundaries and any timezone needed for timestamps. Explain changed windows, late reports and overlap before making year-on-year claims. A brief outlook can remain in the retrospective; a full forward-looking product belongs in [trends-forecasting](<Report-Templates/trends-forecasting.md>).

| Construct | Decision | Boundary / reason |

| Admiralty source and information grading | **Compatible existing core** | Retain per-observation A–F × 1–6 and existing serialisation; no new score or adversary-level source grade. |
| ENISA threat taxonomy / annual categories / sector counting choices | **Not adopted as replacement core vocabularies** | Use source-to-local mappings, preserve loss/unknowns and disclose chart-specific counting rules. |
| ENISA annual probability/confidence-labelled matrix | **Source documented; not adopted as a core scale** | Do not import thresholds into STIX confidence or claim LCA/WEP adoption. |
| EU relevance score, threat/mitigation scoring or severity model | **Not adopted** | No formula, new numeric priority, scoring engine or new severity dimension follows from this alignment. Existing [deliberate gaps](<Patchwork-CTI-Framework.md#25-not-adopted>) remain explicit. |
| AI-derived trends, conclusions and assessments | **Not adopted; excluded from the aligned workflow** | Monitoring/collection and limited editorial assistance only, with accountable human validation and final approval. |
| ENISA publication medium, transport/platform and future automation | **Not adopted as implementation requirements** | Markdown profile and existing STIX model remain. No TAXII transport, new platform/feed integration or automated analysis capability is implied by source discussion or future-work aspirations. |
| ENISA certification, endorsement, mandatory-standard compliance | **No claim** | Methodological borrowing is an editorial, reviewable claim. Existing STIX requirements remain independently testable; headings and link checks certify neither analytical truth nor compliance. |

## Profile-completion-check

Before calling an annual product methodologically aligned, its human author/reviewer should be able to locate:

- The audience, decision need, intelligence requirements, constituency, selected report type and exact period/cut-off.
- The collection plan, provenance, observation grades, translation/curation rules, source limitations and lawful handling decisions.
- Evidence-backed judgments, alternatives and unknowns; defined ranking criteria, units, denominators and comparison caveats.
- Explicit mapping from source labels to local sectors, activity categories and existing objects without forced attribution or new values.
- An honest AI-use statement and accountable human analytical review, plus an audience-appropriate set of recommendations.
- A stable period-start identity anchor distinct from release/revision dates; reviewed narrative/object references and permitted dissemination.
- A feedback owner/channel, disposition record and correction/update process that routes substantive changes and retractions correctly.

The [annual template](<Report-Templates/annual-threat-landscape.md>) prompts for these records; the [source schema](<Easy-access/ENISA/enisa-threat-landscape-2026-schema.md>) explains their provenance. Completing them is an author/reviewer responsibility, not an automated certification.
